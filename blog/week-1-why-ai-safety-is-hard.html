Week 1 — Why AI Safety is Hard

ASICA Team · 10 October 2026

A recap of our first in-person reading discussion, Unit 1: the technical challenge of AI.

This week the cohort met in person for the first time to discuss the Unit 1 readings, The Technical Challenge with AI, on 10 October 2026.

Unit 1 sets up the central question for the whole fundamentals arc: why building AI we can trust is hard, and why the difficulty is technical, not just a matter of intent. The discussion worked through the readings together and surfaced the questions the cohort wants to carry forward.

This week's discussion

Discussion prompts

  • What makes building trustworthy AI a technical problem, and not just a matter of good intentions?
  • Where is the gap between what AI systems can do and our ability to evaluate them widest right now?
  • Which contributing factors, from unequal access to governance, make safe AI harder to achieve in our context?
From the discussion

Key takeaways

A recurring theme across the room: we should remain aware of the potential risks of AI and the contributing factors that make it difficult to build AI safely, from unequal access to governance.

  • The gap between AI capability and our ability to evaluate it is real, and the difficulty is technical, not just a matter of intent.
  • Unequal access and governance shape who AI helps and who it harms, questions the cohort wants to carry into later units.
  • Guardrails need to cover the whole lifecycle: implementation, deployment, application, and use of AI and affiliated technologies.

AI safety, in the cohort's words

Before the readings, members wrote their own one-sentence definition of AI safety. A sample:

  • "A way of making sure that AI does its intended purpose without having negative effects on society or the environment."
  • "Making AI applications less harmful and more beneficial to users."
  • "The ability to align LLMs in education in our African context."
  • "Ensuring AI systems perform tasks as expected, using ethical means and routes, to give the expected output with minimal harm."
  • "Building AI solutions that are fair and limit harms."

Feedback on the session

  • Great discussions around AI safety and existing initiatives.
  • Enjoyed the session and hearing different perspectives on what AI safety is.
What's next

Looking ahead to Unit 2

Next up is Unit 2, What Is AI Alignment?, where we move from why the problem is hard to how the field frames the goal of building aligned systems. See the full seven-unit arc on the cohort kickoff post.
