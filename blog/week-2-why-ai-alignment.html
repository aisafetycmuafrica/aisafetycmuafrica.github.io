Week 2 — Why AI Alignment?

ASICA Team · 17 October 2026

Unit 2: specifying the right goal is only half the problem. Getting a system to actually pursue it is the other half.

Specifying the right goal is only half the problem. Getting a system to actually pursue that goal once it is trained is the other half, and the two fail in different ways.

This unit separates outer from inner alignment and looks at how things could go wrong even when nobody intends them to. The reading "What is AI alignment?" defines alignment, the problem of getting an AI to do what its creator wants, and introduces the two terms used throughout the course: reward misspecification and goal misgeneralisation. In session, we walk through one catastrophe scenario step by step and ask where a different decision could have broken the chain.

Core readings

Start here

  • What is AI alignment? (Adam Jones). Explains how the term is used, and separates reward misspecification (outer misalignment) from goal misgeneralisation (inner misalignment). 15 min.
  • Specification Gaming: How AI Can Turn Your Wishes Against You (Rational Animations, 2024). Video, 10 min. The rewards we train on are usually only proxies for what we want, which can lead to unintended results.
  • How we could stumble into AI catastrophe (Benjamin Hilton).
  • What risks does AI pose? (BlueDot Impact).
  • Intro to AI Safety (Robert Miles).
  • Why AI Safety Matters: a misuse / misalignment / systemic risk framework (The Safety Apprentice, 2026). Start with the framework, then read the 2026 incidents it describes.
Highly recommended

Go deeper

  • More Is Different for AI (Jacob Steinhardt).
  • Recent Frontier Models Are Reward Hacking (METR, 2025). Even when told to solve a task the intended way, models often do not. 15 min.
Additional readings

If you want more

  • Four Background Claims (Nate Soares). MIRI's case for why this work matters now.
  • Specification gaming: the flip side of AI ingenuity (Krakovna et al., DeepMind, 2020).
  • The alignment problem from a deep learning perspective (Ngo, Chan, Mindermann, 2022).
  • A central AI alignment problem: capabilities generalization, and the sharp left turn (Nate Soares, 2022).
  • Is power-seeking AI an existential risk? (Joseph Carlsmith, 2022).
  • The other alignment problem: mesa-optimisers and inner alignment (Robert Miles).
  • AI: Racing Toward the Brink (Sam Harris and Eliezer Yudkowsky, 2018). A conversation about the alignment problem; links to the full transcript hosted by MIRI.
  • What failure looks like (Paul Christiano, 2019). A world that goes out with a whimper through reward misspecification. Part I is the core argument, closely related to Goodhart's law. 10 min.
  • Why Would AI "Aim" To Defeat Humanity? (Holden Karnofsky, 2022). How the way we train AI could lead it to pursue dangerous, unintended goals.
In session

Discussion prompts

  • Outer alignment (specifying the right goal) versus inner alignment (getting the system to actually pursue it). Which feels more solved to you right now, and which feels further away?
  • Walk through one step in "How we could stumble into AI catastrophe." Was there a single clear stop point, or did it only look avoidable in hindsight?
  • Is the difficulty here specific to deep learning, or would any sufficiently capable optimization process run into the same problem?
