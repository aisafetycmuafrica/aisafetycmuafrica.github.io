Week 3 — Training Safer AI Models

ASICA Team · 24 October 2026

Unit 3: if we cannot write rules for every situation, how do we nudge a model's behaviour in the right direction at all?

If we cannot write rules for every situation, how do we nudge a model's behaviour in the right direction at all?

This unit covers three techniques labs actually use: filtering what a model learns from, teaching it through human feedback, and overseeing systems that are already better than us at specific tasks. Each one works, and each one breaks in its own way. In session, members will explain how they would build an assistant with RLHF and where it would fail, then compare the safety techniques they each studied.

Coming up next discussion

Discussion prompts

  • A 2026 mechanistic study tracked 1,920 training transitions and found aggressive PPO hits a 14.45% localized reward-hacking rate that checkpoint-level averages completely miss. If hacking happens during training and gets smoothed out by the final checkpoint, how much should we trust any single eval number?
  • A startup claims "perfect" input data filtering makes their model completely safe. Using the poisoning research (about 250 documents can poison a model of any size), what's wrong with that claim?
